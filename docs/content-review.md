# Content review against other real estate education (October 2026)

We studied popular real estate investing books, websites, YouTube channels, podcasts, courses and seminars: what draws people to them and, more importantly, what people complain about. Then we checked DeedOwl's content against those findings. Full research notes, with dated, linked evidence: the two research reports behind this summary (books and websites; video, podcasts and courses).

## What people value most

1. Concrete, step-by-step how-to with real numbers (J Scott on flipping, McElroy's valuation steps, Gallinelli's metrics).
2. Honesty about work and risk, including advice that helps someone decide not to invest yet (praised in *Real Estate Investing For Dummies*).
3. State- and county-specific detail ("every county does it differently"); the state tables are the most praised part of tax-lien books.
4. Free tools with no limits, and resentment when calculators are paywalled.
5. Real failure stories and investigations, not just wins.
6. Community and live Q&A.

## What people complain about most

| Rank | Complaint | Worst documented cases |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Free content that funnels into paid workshops and coaching | FTC/Utah cases: Response Marketing (2023, $16.7M), Zurixx (2022), Real Estate Workshop (Utah, 2024); Trump University ($25M) |
| 2 | Being told to borrow (credit cards, credit limits, home equity, retirement funds) to pay for courses or deals | Rich Dad seminars (CBC), Zurixx, Real Estate Workshop |
| 3 | False or unsupported return claims ("guaranteed", "16–50%", "passive") | Tax-lien marketers; lien rates are bid down at auction |
| 4 | Vague, padded, motivational writing instead of how-to | Rich Dad, several popular books |
| 5 | Risk played down; survivorship bias | Podcast success stories, "zero risk" titles |
| 6 | Outdated numbers and rules | Pre-2008 and pre-COVID figures in popular books |
| 7 | Generic national advice that ignores state and county rules | Most national creators |
| 8 | Conflicts of interest and undisclosed pay | Sponsored crypto mentions, creators selling their own funds |
| 9 | Risky tactics taught to beginners (wholesaling, "subject-to") | Seller harm and new state rules |
| 10 | Forum noise, spam and conflicted "advice" | Turnkey sellers answering beginners |

Utah, one of DeedOwl's states, is the hub of these enforcement cases.

## How DeedOwl measures up

**Already doing well**
- Free, with no paid tier, coaching, upsells or calculator limits.
- No hype words: a scan found no "guaranteed", "passive income", "zero risk" or "risk-free" claims. The calculators say "Not a guarantee."
- State- and county-specific rules, a state filter so each visitor sees only their state, and sources on every rule.
- Review dates on lessons and guides.
- Real stories of what went wrong as well as what went right (new property type and professional pages).
- Risky tactics covered with their downside: the creative financing lesson explains the due-on-sale risk of "subject-to" deals and warns about "no money down" seminars, citing the FTC/Utah case.
- The Decide quiz can already recommend "Learn first: practice and paper deals."
- The Amazon Associates disclosure is in fine print where affiliate links appear.

**Where to improve (in priority order)**

1. **A "Seminar red flags" page.** Built from the enforcement record: free event, then paid workshop, then $10K–$65K coaching; pressure to raise credit limits or borrow; "guaranteed" returns; gag clauses. Link the FTC and the Utah Division of Consumer Protection complaint sites. Utah is in our coverage area.
2. **An explicit "don't borrow to learn or to bid" warning** in the financing and creative financing lessons and on the auction guides.
3. **Tax-sale surplus money.** Explain on Homeowner help that surplus from a tax or foreclosure sale belongs to the former owner and lienholders, who can claim it directly from the county, and warn about "finders" who charge large fees. Extend the no-owner-contact rule to surplus-fund outreach.
4. **Realistic lien returns in Arizona.** State that winning rates are bid down, often far below 16%, and that most liens are paid off with interest rather than ending in a deed. The Arizona guide already says this in part.
5. **A readiness check in Decide.** Make "not yet" a clear result when someone has no emergency fund, high-interest debt or very little cash, and point them to practice first.
6. **A risk box on each strategy in Decide:** worst realistic outcome, cash at risk, and typical time to a first deal.
7. **Worked examples with real numbers, including a losing deal,** for each strategy and sale type. The guides have fictional examples; the lessons need more.
8. **Wholesaling.** If covered at all, as an advanced, high-risk practice with the seller-harm cases and each state's rules (Arizona requires written disclosure; California's licensing bill is pending). Needs statute checks first.
9. **A visible "last reviewed" date and a corrections log** on every page, not only lessons and guides.
10. **Keep pages short:** one idea per section and a clear next step, to avoid the "padded" complaint.

## What we will never do

No paid coaching or seminars, no deal syndication, no paid placement among official listing sources, no earnings or return promises, no celebrity or lifestyle imagery, no urgency tactics, and no advice to borrow on credit to learn or to bid.
